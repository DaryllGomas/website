01The problem

The work ends. The paperwork doesn't.

After every client visit there are notes to write, an invoice to make, a PDF to file, an email to send and, later, a payment to record. Commercial PSA suites (the ticketing-and-billing tools IT firms run on) handle this for bigger shops. At a previous IT job I helped evaluate one, set up parts of it and used it every day.

A one-person business needs the same records, kept just as carefully, without that overhead. And the paperwork shouldn't eat the time that should go to the next client.

"I'm taking notes in the terminal … and at the end I just wrap it up."

02What I built

My own office system, run with an AI.

  • Clients. Rates, contact details and status from prospect to active, with a next follow-up, an activity log and a "due today" list.
  • Jobs and invoices. Hours × rate, calculated by the server. PDF invoices with a card-payment link and QR code.
  • Money. An income ledger with one row per payment. Checks and part payments are recorded against the invoice; card payments through Stripe are built to record themselves.
  • Operations. A lead pipeline, a business scorecard and CSV export.
  • Client PCs. A Devices tab for each client, wired to Heimdall for Add a PC and Connect.
  • An AI operator. A Claude Code session runs the visit paperwork through the app's own API.

Python and Flask, one SQLite file, PDFs drawn with ReportLab. It runs on my own server on my private network and is backed up every night.

The guardrails

Routine

The same front door

For invoices and payments the AI uses the app's own API from my signed-in browser, so every rule the app applies to me applies to it.

Routine

Nothing reaches a client unchecked

I approve the notes and the email text first. The email lands in my drafts, and I send it myself.

App rule

Sent means locked

A sent or paid invoice can't be edited or deleted. Taking one back needs an explicit unlock, and every change of state is written to an audit trail.

Routine + app rule

Logged, with undo steps

Each wrap-up is logged with the exact steps to reverse it. A mistaken payment is voided with a reason, never deleted.

App rule

Money counted once

Card payment notices are accepted only with Stripe's signature, and a repeated notice can't record the same payment twice.

App rule

One owner, one network

One owner login, with wrong guesses limited. Changes are only accepted from the app's own pages. The app isn't on the public internet, and it's backed up nightly.

"App rule" means the app enforces it for anyone, AI or me. "Routine" means it is how every wrap-up is run.

An invoice's life

Draft · editable→ Sent · locked→ Paid · locked

Going back to draft needs a deliberate unlock, and the step is recorded.

06How it's tested and run

Seven gates before any change goes live.

The app holds the money, so since October 2026 every change to it goes through the same seven gates.

  1. BaselineA backup of the live data, plus a snapshot of every read-only screen and report, saved before anything changes.
  2. A change map I approveThe exact code before and after, everything it touches, the data it changes counted on a copy, and how to roll it back.
  3. Red, then greenEach fix gets a test that must fail on the old code and pass on the new.
  4. Before and after, comparedThe full suite, then the snapshot rerun on a fresh copy of the live data. Every difference must be one the map predicted.
  5. An independent reviewCodex checks the whole change against the map: correct, secure, and nothing more than the map says.
  6. Live checkA fresh backup, then a click-through of every main screen, and live counts compared with the map. Then I look.
  7. RecordA changelog entry, the commits, and rollback steps tested on a copy.
